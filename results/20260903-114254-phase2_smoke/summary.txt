  ties: 13 of 64 voted bits split evenly across frames and were
  decided on the summed decision margin rather than resolved to 0. An even
  frame count is what makes that possible; a high tie rate is also a symptom,
  since frames only disagree this much when there is nothing for them to agree on.

  BER over payload bits, decoded at the sync the receiver recovered.
  BER|snc is the same decode on the TRUE chip grid, so it measures
  demodulation with acquisition removed; acq says whether the recovered
  sync landed within half a chip. A run with acq=NO has a BER at chance
  whatever the channel did, so read BER and BER|snc together: they part
  company exactly where the failure is acquisition and not noise. |pk| is
  the winning correlation, the acquisition statistic a real receiver could
  compute for itself -- it has no sync column to check. Peak over best
  sidelobe, the obvious alternative, does not separate the two at all.
  pre = preamble BER (a sync check);
  vote = BER after majority vote across frames; dW and sd are
  the per-chip separation and noise, d-marg their ratio. d-pair is
  the same for the within-symbol difference the decision actually
  uses, and Q(pair) is the error rate it predicts -- compare that
  with BER, not d-marg, which ignores noise shared between chips.
  ovr% is the share of RAPL edges seen late; sync = recovered
  start minus true start, in chips.

  acq       : repeats whose recovered sync landed within half a chip.
  BER|snc   : mean BER demodulated on the true chip grid. Where this is
              far below BER, the rate was limited by acquisition and not
              by per-chip noise, and the remedy is a better preamble
              rather than a slower symbol.
  cap b/s   : raw rate x (1 - H(BER)), the BSC capacity at the aggregated
              BER. This is the rate/reliability figure to quote and compare
              against the literature; BER at a rate is not one number.
  vote b/s  : raw / frames, the rate actually delivered after the majority
              vote, since the F frames carry one payload between them.
  bits d/tx : distinct payload bits / times they were transmitted.
  p(msg)    : the honest significance -- the message-space null on the voted
              decode. Each repeat is one distinct balanced message, so it is
              one independent truth however many frames carried it; the null
              holds the (possibly biased, self-correlated) decode fixed and
              asks how often a random truth would match it as well. r perfect
              repeats of m balanced bits give (1/C(m,m/2))**r.
  p(tx)     : the binomial over transmissions, kept only as the INFLATED
              upper bound. It counts every repeat of a bit as an independent
              trial, which overstates the evidence by orders of magnitude at
              small payloads -- 1.8e-18 against p(msg)'s 2.9e-6 on the
              eight-bit headline row. Quote p(msg).
